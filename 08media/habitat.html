Habitat

The Right Enclosure

Sugar gliders need a tall, spacious cage rather than a small enclosure. Because they spend a lot of time climbing and moving vertically, height is especially important. Avoid cages with large gaps between the bars. Sugar gliders can squeeze through surprisingly small spaces, especially young gliders.
Look for a cage with:

  • Plenty of vertical space for climbing
  • Narrow bar spacing to prevent escapes or injuries
  • A secure door and latches
  • Non-toxic, durable materials
  • Enough room for toys, sleeping pouches, food dishes, and a wheel

Location

Place the cage in a quiet area of your home where your glider can still see and interact with the family. Keep it away from direct sunlight, drafts, loud noises, and heating or cooling vents. Because sugar gliders are nocturnal, they will usually sleep during the day and become active in the evening and at night.

Sleeping Area

Sugar gliders need a comfortable place to sleep and hide. Fleece sleeping pouches are a popular choice and give them a warm, secure space to rest. Check pouches regularly for loose threads, holes, or other damage that could cause injuries.

Climbing and Enrichment

Sugar gliders are natural climbers and need toys and accessories that let them climb, jump, forage, and explore. Safe options include sturdy natural branches, fleece ropes, ladders, bridges, hammocks, tunnels, and platforms. Make sure branches are untreated and free from pesticides or harmful chemicals.

For play and mental stimulation, you can also provide foraging toys, treat puzzles, hanging toys, and appropriately designed exercise wheels. Rotate toys regularly to keep the habitat interesting and prevent boredom.

Clean and Safe

Clean the habitat regularly by removing waste and uneaten food and washing food and water dishes. Check the cage and accessories for broken parts, sharp edges, or loose materials. Keeping the habitat clean, secure, and stimulating will help your sugar glider feel safe and comfortable.
